# ITC — analyst research notes

## Executive conclusion

**Status: WATCH — investable business, but the purchase decision is not complete.**

ITC combines an exceptional legal-cigarette franchise, a very large distribution system, a growing branded-consumer portfolio and a net-cash balance sheet. The central analytical caution is concentration: cigarettes supplied approximately **82% of FY26 segment result**, even though they represented only about **45% of external segment revenue**. Diversification is real in products and revenue, but not yet in profit economics.

The immediate decision variable is the cigarette tax reset effective February 2026. ITC has historically demonstrated pricing power, but the FY27 response of legal volumes, illicit substitution and margins cannot be inferred reliably from one post-change quarter. At the approximate 27 July 2026 market reference of ₹286, the shares imply roughly **17.3x FY26 continuing EPS**, a **5.1% dividend yield** and a **4.5% approximate free-cash-flow yield**. Those figures look reasonable, not conclusive: a scenario-based sum-of-parts valuation and evidence of post-tax resilience are still required.

## Evidence boundary and comparability

Primary analysis is based on the supplied audited **ITC Report and Accounts 2026**. Market price and market capitalisation are dated external references, not audited company facts. Category leadership claims are company disclosures that cite NielsenIQ/Kantar; they are stronger than unsupported assertions but do not replace complete independent share histories.

FY25 total profit is distorted by a ₹15,016 crore discontinued-operations contribution related to the Hotels demerger, including a large exceptional gain. Comparisons in this memo therefore use **continuing PAT: ₹21,018 crore in FY26 versus ₹20,036 crore in FY25**. Treating total PAT as recurring would be a serious analytical error.

## Financial performance and accounting quality

| Continuing operations | FY25 ₹ crore | FY26 ₹ crore | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue from operations | 81,612.78 | 89,913.33 | 10.2% |
| PBT before exceptional items | 26,926.94 | 28,325.09 | 5.2% |
| PAT | 20,036.47 | 21,018.15 | 4.9% |
| CFO | 17,627.04 | 18,464.31 | 4.8% |
| Approximate FCF (CFO less asset purchases) | 15,348.34 | 16,281.42 | 6.1% |

Revenue growth was healthy, but profit conversion weakened. Continuing PAT grew less than half as fast as revenue. That is consistent with cigarette margin pressure, Paperboards weakness and cost inflation—not with an unqualified claim of improving operating economics.

The auditor issued an unmodified consolidated opinion. The only consolidated key audit matter was revenue recognition/cut-off. Internal financial controls were reported adequate and operating effectively. No evidence in the supplied report justifies a financial-statement manipulation allegation.

## Cash-flow quality

FY26 CFO of ₹18,464 crore was strong in absolute terms but equalled only about **0.88x continuing PAT**. Operating profit before working-capital movements rose about 3.3%, while inventory and biological assets absorbed roughly ₹3,306 crore. Receivables released cash, so inventory—not customer collection—was the main working-capital drag.

Approximate FCF was ₹16,281 crore, while cash dividends paid were ₹18,271 crore. One year of dividends above approximate FCF is affordable given the investment-rich balance sheet, but it should not automatically be labelled fully covered. The next report must establish whether inventory normalises and whether the payout remains covered over a cycle.

## Balance sheet and capital allocation

Owner equity was ₹72,507 crore. Gross borrowings rose to roughly ₹2,186 crore, predominantly current, but ITC held more than ₹32,000 crore in current/non-current investments plus material bank balances. The correct economic characterization is **net cash**, not “debt increased, therefore leverage risk.” The rise in gross borrowing should nevertheless be reconciled rather than ignored.

ITC paid ₹18,168 crore of ordinary dividends for FY26 and has a long record of large distributions. The Hotels demerger improved structural focus while retaining a 39.85% associate interest. The harder capital-allocation question is not balance-sheet safety; it is whether acquisitions, incubated brands and non-cigarette capex earn attractive incremental returns. The annualised revenue of newer businesses above ₹1,350 crore is evidence of scale, not proof of ROIC.

### Cigarettes

The franchise has extraordinary economics: high repeat purchase, brand habit, legal distribution scale, route-to-market density and substantial pricing power. Its vulnerability is externally imposed: taxation, illicit trade and regulation. FY26 cigarette revenue grew 13.1%, but segment result grew only 5.5% and result margin fell from about 58.8% to 54.8%. The February 2026 tax increase creates a fresh elasticity test; management’s warning about illicit trade is plausible but remains a forward claim.

### FMCG-Others

This is the key diversification engine. Revenue grew 10.5% and segment profit 13.9%, with margin improving to about 7.45% from 7.23%. Aashirvaad, Bingo!, Sunfeast, Classmate and YiPPee! have leading category positions according to industry data cited in the report. Nearly seven million outlets, over 40% direct servicing and UNNATI’s 800,000-plus outlet network form a meaningful distribution advantage.

However, most packaged-food categories have low consumer switching costs and intense advertising/promotion. Distribution improves availability and launch economics; it does not by itself create cigarette-like pricing power. The thesis requires sustained profit growth above revenue growth and evidence that brand investment is generating incremental returns.

### Agri and Paperboards

Agri links sourcing, exports and the broader food portfolio, while ITCMAARS may improve farmer engagement and traceability. It is exposed to regulation, crop availability and commodity cycles. Paperboards benefits from integration and packaging capability but suffered a 14.6% segment-profit decline despite revenue growth. That divergence is a red flag to investigate: input costs, realisations, capacity, imports and structural demand must be separated.

## Customer and moat analysis

- **Who pays?** Consumers pay for cigarettes and packaged goods; enterprises buy paper, packaging and agri products; distributors and retailers control shelf access.
- **Why buy?** Cigarettes depend on brand preference and habit. Food and personal-care purchases depend on availability, trust, taste, price and promotion. B2B purchases emphasize specification, consistency, service and price.
- **Can customers switch?** Cigarette brand switching is relatively sticky but not impossible. FMCG switching costs are generally low. Industrial customers can switch after qualification, subject to performance and supply reliability.
- **Who holds channel power?** ITC’s direct distribution reduces reliance on intermediaries, but retailers still allocate shelf space and competitors can buy visibility.
- **Moat conclusion:** The strongest defensibility comes from the combined system—brands, route-to-market, retailer data, agri sourcing, manufacturing and R&D. No precise replication cost or time is evidenced, so fabricated figures are prohibited.

## Management and governance

Management scores well on disclosure, balance-sheet conservatism, portfolio building and dividend distributions. The financial statements contain detailed segments, risk factors and a ten-year history. Corporate-governance compliance was reported, and the audit opinion was unmodified.

The unresolved management question is incremental capital allocation. “Nearly 100 launches” demonstrates activity, not productivity. Acquisitions and incubations need cohort-level revenue, margin and capital-return evidence. Multi-year management-promise tracking is not yet mature enough to grade guidance accuracy; this report establishes the baseline instead of inventing a score.

## Investment debate

### Strongest ownership case

ITC owns a highly profitable, cash-generative tobacco franchise and uses its distribution, sourcing and brand-building capabilities to construct a broader consumer platform. FMCG-Others is now showing positive operating leverage, the balance sheet is net cash, distributions are large, and the reference valuation is far below typical premium-consumer multiples.

### Strongest rejection case

The group remains overwhelmingly dependent on cigarette profit precisely when taxation has reset upward. Legal-volume erosion or sustained margin compression would overwhelm near-term profit improvements elsewhere. FMCG-Others earns much lower margins, Paperboards is weakening, FY26 cash conversion was qualified by inventory, and diversification ROIC is not yet disclosed convincingly.

### Neutral assessment

This is not a low-quality company and not a simple “cheap tobacco” security. It is a regulated cash franchise funding a consumer-platform transition. The correct decision is determined by price, the durability of legal-cigarette economics and the rate at which non-cigarette profit—not merely revenue—compounds.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Operating path | Decision implication |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Bull | 25% | Cigarette pricing absorbs the tax reset with limited legal-volume damage; FMCG-Others margin moves above 8%; inventory normalises. | Earnings resilience and visible diversification could justify a higher multiple. |
| Base | 50% | Cigarettes grow modestly after adjustment; FMCG profit compounds from a small base; Paper/Agri remain cyclical; dividends continue. | Current reference valuation looks reasonable, but entry still requires a satisfactory SOTP range. |
| Bear | 25% | Illicit substitution and price elasticity weaken cigarette volumes/margins; FMCG needs heavier brand spend; cash conversion stays soft. | Tobacco de-rating would dominate dividends and non-cigarette growth. |

These probabilities are explicit analyst priors, not statistical forecasts. They must be recalibrated against FY27 evidence.

## Valuation

Using an approximate market capitalisation of ₹3.58 trillion dated 27 July 2026 and FY26 continuing metrics:

- Approximate share price: ₹285.7.
- Continuing P/E: 17.3x.
- Price/book: approximately 5.0x.
- Dividend yield using ₹14.50 per share: approximately 5.1%.
- Approximate FCF yield: 4.5%.

These are reference ratios, not intrinsic value. A credible valuation must separately value cigarettes with a regulatory/terminal-growth discount, FMCG-Others using sustainable margins, Agri/Paper cyclically, net investments/cash, and the retained Hotels associate. No margin of safety is declared until that work is complete.

## Fact / inference / projection log

- **Audited fact:** FY26 continuing revenue ₹89,913 crore, PAT ₹21,018 crore and CFO ₹18,464 crore.
- **Audited fact:** Cigarettes produced approximately 82% of segment result.
- **Audited fact:** Inventory and biological assets absorbed approximately ₹3,306 crore of operating cash.
- **Management claim:** The tax increase risks strengthening illicit cigarette trade.
- **Analyst inference:** Distribution is a genuine advantage, but its economic strength differs by category.
- **Analyst inference:** Profit diversification materially lags revenue diversification.
- **Projection:** Base-case continuing earnings growth remains positive in FY27; confidence is only medium-low until post-tax data arrives.

## Decision-linked research agenda

1. Reconstruct FY27 legal cigarette volume from disclosed revenue, pricing and category data.
2. Build a true SOTP under bull/base/bear tobacco multiples and FMCG margin outcomes.
3. Reconcile the FY26 inventory build by segment and determine whether it reverses.
4. Track FMCG-Others margin and acquisition-level profitability.
5. Compare category market-share changes with independent NielsenIQ/Kantar data when obtainable.
6. Diagnose Paperboards margin compression as cyclical versus structural.
7. Review related-party transactions, executive pay and capital employed in acquired brands in depth.
