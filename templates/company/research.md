# Research notes

## Business model

Start with the economic system, not the ratios. How does the company make money? Describe products, customers, geographies, segments, revenue drivers, cost structure, and reinvestment requirements.

## Customer and purchase decision

For every important customer group: who pays, who uses, who influences/specifies, why they buy, what causes switching, how price-sensitive they are, whether demand repeats, and who holds channel power.

## Competitive position

What is the moat? For every claimed advantage record the economic mechanism, beneficiary, counter-evidence, replication requirements, time, cost, reasons replication may fail, durability, and confidence. Never invent replication estimates.

## Industry and competitors

Map suitable competitors, capacity, demand, pricing, substitution, regulation, technology, and market-position history. Use dated primary external evidence; company claims are not independent validation.

## Management and governance

Record promoter holding, pledging, auditor observations, related-party transactions, capital allocation, remuneration, and management credibility.

Assess operating execution, incremental returns, guidance accuracy, acquisitions, innovation productivity, culture and retention, compensation alignment, minority treatment, succession, disclosure quality, and response to mistakes.

## Management discussion and outlook

Summarize management’s claims. Keep claims distinct from verified facts.

## Risks

List business, financial, industry, regulatory, customer, supplier, currency, and governance risks.

For each material driver, trace the direct effect followed by competitor, customer, channel, capital-allocation, and long-term-equilibrium responses.
